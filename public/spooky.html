The Flaky Test Monster

A spooky story for Syntax · Halloween 2026

The code that turned into music. Twice.

Some bugs you fix. Some bugs you lose. And some come back, years later, singing.

Chapter one · somewhere in the 2000s

The source that vanished

It was the age of Macromedia Flash. Intros you couldn’t skip, buttons that wobbled, and everything lived in one precious .fla file: the timeline, the tweens, the ActionScript, the sounds.

Then one day the source was gone. No backup, no version control, no git reflog to crawl back into. What was left was a compiled .swf that still played but could never be edited again. The software was dead.

But something in it would not stay buried. What could no longer be code became music: an alternative electronic record with the brass and the restless rhythms of the Bukovina, which went on to become a hit.

From software to music. Nobody planned it. Nobody could have.

Chapter two · October 2023 · Kibana 8.11

Something in the pipeline

Every change to Kibana runs through thousands of automated tests on CI. Some of them fail although nothing changed. Rerun them and they pass. Those are flaky tests, and at scale they never stop coming.

In the chat of the Discover team, someone noticed that while kertal was on holiday, fewer flaky tests came in. Suspicious. Somebody had to be making them, so the culprit got a face: the Flaky Test Monster, a little green goblin handcrafting flaky tests with love.

Passed locally, failed on CI.

Chapter three · character design and 3D printing

It crawled out of the screen

A joke became a character: spiky black hair, googly eyes, a mouth too wide for its face. The character became a 3D model, and the model came off the printer, layer by layer, into the physical world.

The team tried everything to get rid of it. It was sent on holiday to Italy, Croatia, the Austrian Alps, Spain and Las Vegas. It came back. It got a LinkedIn account, Open to work. The account was blocked. Prints and buttons were given away to other teams. No reduction in flakiness observed. Every measure was flaky.

In October 2026 it was exhibited at the 20th anniversary of the Happylab makerspace in Vienna. It even inspired a tool that turns anyone’s face into a printable monster.

Chapter four · and in the end: music

The monster wants to sing

And then, just like twenty years before, the code found its way into music. The Flaky Test Monster is going for the Eurovision Song Slong Contest. All it needs is a country brave enough to send it, so it recorded From Flaky With Love in every candidate’s style: Austrian, Italian, French, Greek, Spanish, Polish… even Klingon, and in cat.

Listen and vote for its country

The moral

Lose your source and you may get a hit record. Ignore your flaky tests and they grow teeth, get printed and enter Eurovision. Software doesn’t die. It just changes genre.

Back up your .fla files. Fix your flaky tests. Or don’t, and see what happens.
